# Iron Age, concept C: "The Apparatus Catalogue"

V59 Phase D, design panel, slot `iron-age`, angle C. Written 2026-09-27 by the concept-C agent. Nothing here is approved, and nothing in either tree was touched.

- **Name in the picker:** Iron Age. **Feel line:** "Ink on cream, circa 1900." (25 characters).
- **id:** `iron-age`. **scheme:** `light`. **kind:** deep + art. **Layout rule:** same order, new shapes.
- **The idea, in two sentences.** Rack is set like a gymnasium-apparatus and scale catalogue of 1891–1919: ink on a groundwood cream stock, every object shown as an engraved "cut", every bold word in Clarendon and every plain word in a Modern book roman, the way those catalogues paired them. The engraved cut set is the signature — the dock, the gears, the lock, the eight add-food cuts, the carafe and a printers' fist in place of v1's sparkle — and structure comes from hairline boxes, ruled cells and double rules instead of cards.

## 1. The model, and what carries over

The catalogues (research track 8b §1, G1, G6; track 7 §1 items H, I):

- **Spalding c. 1891** gymnasium catalogue: rings, clubs, dumbbells and a gymnasium beam scale, each an unsigned engraved profile "cut" with the item name in a bold face and the price in a ruled column.
- **Fairbanks 1867 / 1919** scale catalogues: platform and counter scales, a thin–thick–thin page frame, an italic caption in a hairline box, a stamped nameplate.
- **Sears No. 112 (c. 1902)**: the fork, tumbler, carafe, nib, box camera, calendar stand and insole cuts.
- **Polhemus 1895** stock cuts: the printers' fist.
- **Ravenstein & Hulley 1867**: the globe bar-bell woodcut (Tier A, #29).

What transfers, look-only:

| Catalogue device | Rack element |
|---|---|
| The engraved "cut" of an object in profile | Every icon, and one tailpiece object per screen |
| Bold Clarendon for names and prices, a Modern roman for descriptions (Clarendon was "the first type actually designed as a 'related bold'", T5 §5.3) | Besley for every bold word and every figure; Old Standard TT for every plain word |
| The ruled grid of cuts, each with its name under it | The add-food tiles (`addTile · ruled`) |
| The hairline caption box | Every stamp: chips, tags, plate chips, the session plates, the estimator notice, the lead plate |
| The halftone plate with its keyline | The lead box of a tab, with its photo in a band at the top and the box's own words below it, never over it |
| Item-division double rules | The head rule on every card and sheet |
| Price columns with leaders | Settings only: the order blank |

### 2.12 Contract additions this concept needs (it does not invent them; the orchestrator rules)

1. **Two families** (Q-E3 "a figure face"): `face.display` + `face.text`, and a `face` key on each type preset. Every Iron Age concept needs this.
2. **`band`** colour role (R3.3).
3. **`inkOf`**: the text ink per plate role, `{ pYellow: 'warn', pChrome: 'steel' }`. Text sites that take a group or subject colour resolve through it. R2.3 and T7 G.6 require it on a card-less light page.
4. **`shape`** params (VOCAB §4, orchestrator's call).
5. **`card · ruled` lead-box keyline:** `shape.lead: { keyline: true }` draws the lead box as a catalogue plate (plate paper inside a 1 pt ink keyline). Without it the lead box is plate paper alone, only 1.06:1 over the stock.
6. **Image band mode:** `images.<slot>.band` (pt). The photo is drawn only in a band at the top of the hero box, with the box's padding raised by that much, so no word ever sits on the photo (§6). The engine's `build()` refuses a hero photo with no scrim, so band mode needs the same kind of allowance as D-H3's baked scrim.
7. **`vessel`** field in the icon set (8b G6.2), carrying its own `bottom` / `top`.
8. **`ornaments`** field in the icon set, and a tailpiece site per tab (web: a `::after` with `content: ''` and an SVG background on the screen's last block; native: a footer View). Always `aria-hidden` / `accessible={false}`.
9. **Web SVG `<pattern>` defs** for `chart · print`'s hatches (VOCAB §8.6), and the hero-slot hooks (VOCAB §8.2).
10. **Coach metrics** through `T.fit` (`coach-face.json`), plus Old Standard tables for the `verify-custom-movement` and `verify-estimate-row` surfaces, which measure Archivo 600 today (V59 §6.6).

### 3.1 The one hard rule this concept cannot meet literally

**"Web fonts must be variable with a wght axis."** Old Standard TT has no variable build. This concept is built on it (its angle is IA-2), so C:

- uses it at **one weight only** (400) on both clients, so no role ever needs its weight to change;
- routes every bold word to Besley, which is variable. That is how the period paired a Modern with a Clarendon;
- declares its `@font-face` over `font-weight: 100 900` with `font-synthesis: none`, so the stylesheet keeps **zero `font-weight` rules**, and any `font-variation-settings` on it is simply ignored.

Track 5 already lists "Old Standard (static, used at 400 for reading text)" as an accepted exception to its own variable rule [T5 §0]. **Micah or the orchestrator rules.**

**The fallback if refused:** the text face becomes Archivo (as in IA-1), and the Coach card keeps v1's metrics. Everything else in C is unchanged: palette, Besley, icons, cuts, rules, band photos.

### 3.2 The Coach card in Old Standard

- **Face:** the whole card in `OldStandard-Regular`: the line 14, the reason 12, the greeting 15.
- **Metrics:** `coach-face.json`: `{ face: { chars, line, why }, type: { title: {11, 15}, goT: {11, 15} } }`. Padding 14 and border 1 are unchanged.
- **Only the two 10 pt rows move, to 11 pt** (R4.2). That leaves `cardLayout()`'s line budgets identical at scale 1 and at 1.6, on You and on Train (measured with the real code).
- **Measured with `coach-view.js`'s own `cardLayout()` and `textLines()`,** sliced verbatim from rack-mobile. The corpus was 16 lines and 4 reasons, at widths 320 / 375 / 390 / 430, scales 1 / 1.235 / 1.353 / 1.6, on You and on Train, 640 cases in all:
  - 604 cases wrap the same;

- **No photo on the Coach card** (C27): its caution, loading and accent colours would need a scrim so heavy the photo keeps only 1.07–1.16:1.

## 4. Shape language

- **Square, thin, inked.** Radius 0 everywhere except the avatar. No shadows. No glass. No gradients.
- **Three line weights, all in ink:**
  - the 0.5 pt hairline, for rows and cells;
  - the 1 pt keyline, for stamps, plates and caption boxes;
  - the double rule (0.95 / 1.3 / 0.95), for heads.
  
  A 2 pt rule appears once, on the dock.
- **Four container roles (R6.1):**
  - **lead:** the plate, one per tab: plate paper in a keyline, its photo in a band;
  - **group:** a card that hangs from a double rule, no ground;
  - **callout:** a hairline caption box, such as the estimator notice or the "Next week" box;
  - **sheet:** edge to edge, square, under the double rule.

  Only the lead plate has both a fill and a border.
- **Rhythm (R6.8):** 6 within a group, 18 between cards, 36 before a section head. The lead plate is padded 16; list rows sit on a 44 pt pitch.
- **One surprise per tab (R6.9):** the lead plate's band and its Condensed figure.

## 7. Image slots, the band, and the photographs

### 7.1 Band mode (C's answer to "photos only survive under ink")

Research proved that under any scrim holding 4.5:1, a photo keeps at most 3.10:1 of its own tone under ink text and 1.15:1 under the accent [T8a H.2]. That leaves two choices: single-ink slots, or no text on the photo.

C takes the catalogue's own answer, **the cut above the caption**:

- In each chosen hero box, the photo fills an **80 pt band at the top** of the plate.
- The box's padding rises by 80, so every word, control and colour stays exactly as it is, below it, on plate paper.
- No word is drawn on the photo. That has four effects:
  - the photo keeps its **full 13.26:1** ink-on-stock tone;
  - the greeting's name, the zone colours and the green of Steps all keep their roles, so there is no D-H1 trade-off;
  - there is no runtime scrim and no iOS compositing question (D-H5);
  - the Steps ring never meets a photo (D-H8).
- In the engine's own scrim shape this is a hard-stop split (C22): stock at α 0 over the band and α 1 under the words (addition 6).
- An empty or failed slot closes up: no band, no frame (C14).

## 10. Icons: the signature

### 10.1 Style guide

- **The grid:** 24 units, `fill: 'none'`, one ink stroke, **square caps and miter joins** (v1 is round and round). Flattened to `path` / `circle` / `rect`.
- **Stroke:**
  - 1.5 at 19–22 pt sites;
  - 1.75 at 14–17 pt (gears, calendar, bubble, lock, glyphs);
  - the three sites that fix their own keep them: the FAB 2.6, spark 1.6, the dock from the vibe's CSS (1.5).
- **Drawing:** each object in the catalogue's profile or elevation. Silhouette plus one or two defining lines: the globe's highlight arc, the scale's poise, the nib's slit. **No hatching at icon sizes**; no aged edges; no lettering or numerals.
- **Two kinds of icon:**
  - *Traced* icons cite their 8b entry. The paths in `icons-draft.mjs` are provisional drawings to each source's proportions; Phase V replaces each with the recorded `imagetracerjs` trace of that crop plus hand simplification, keeping this grid, stroke, caps and element count.
  - *Hand-drawn* icons follow 8b G3's rules and are never in PROVENANCE.

## 11. What it never does

1. Never a sparkle, star or bolt. `spark` is the printers' fist.
2. Never tracked caps. C has no caps role at all; only the authored COACH ME is capitals.
3. Never an accented word in a headline: the greeting's name is ink.
4. Never a box on every section: only the lead plate and sheets have a ground, and only the plate has both fill and border.
5. Never more than one ornament per screen, never an ornament inside data, never Victorian scrollwork, crossed dumbbells, wreaths, ribbons or "EST." badges.
6. Never a word on a photograph. Never a photo on the Coach card, Start workout or Fuel. Never an identifiable or named person as a hero. Never lettering, a "Fig." label or a trade name from any plate or cut.
7. Never CSS `sepia()`, stains, foxing, vignettes, torn edges, fibres, letterpress "bite" or exaggerated wander. Grain is measured (σ 0.30 %) and on the page only.
8. Never Old Standard at display size (≤ 16 pt), never at a second weight, and never Besley for an arrowed string.
9. Never radius (the avatar excepted), never a shadow, never glass, never a gradient wash.
10. Never up or down by red against green alone: every delta keeps its arrow or sign.
11. Never ochre or pewter as small text (`inkOf`).
12. Never hatching inside an icon, a date on the calendar cut, or a carafe whose level isn't v1's number.
13. Never wood type, a Didone display face, Rye, Tuscans, blackletter, drop caps or centred data.
14. Never a cream in the AI band, a clay or emerald accent, or a serif italic accent word.
15. Never a delta pill (the pill tints are 0), and never a stat tile.
16. Never new motion: no ink-in, no rule that draws itself, no count-up.
17. Never a light status band on the web, and never a flash of cream before the account's vibe is known.
18. Never an icon library, SF Symbols or emoji. Every icon is a traced cut or drawn to the house rules.
19. Never SPALDING, TRADE MARK, LEX. G. WORKS, M.W.&Co., Milo, YMCA or Pathé lettering.
20. Never the dock's current tab told by colour alone (the rail bar and the heavier label), and never a change to its tabs, order or place.
21. Never madder on a button, a link, the dock or a second place on one screen.
